Polo Skeleton

A study of Piaget's openworked Polo, rebuilt part by part in code. It keeps your time under your own sky.

Take it apart

Six and a half millimetres.

Eighteen-carat rose gold, 42 mm across and 6.5 mm thin with the movement inside. Brushed across the bezel, polished on every chamfer.

A cushion inside a circle.

The Polo's signature: a round case with a cushion-shaped opening. The flange widens into each corner, and the minute track follows the curve.

The dial is the movement.

There is no dial. The calibre 1200S1 is cut away and coated blue, so the barrel, the wheels and the balance show from the front.

Everything inside 2.4 millimetres.

31.9 × 2.4 mm

Calibre 1200S1: 189 parts, 25 jewels, 44 hours of reserve, six beats a second. Its tooth counts are unpublished, so these wheels are modelled.

A rotor set into the plate.

The micro-rotor sits flush inside the movement instead of on top of it. That is how the whole watch stays 6.5 mm thin.

Drag the watch and the rotor swings with gravity, as it would on a wrist.

Back together, still on time.

The sky behind the watch is the real sky where you are, right now.
